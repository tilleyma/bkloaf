Step by step · Part 1

Mixing

Wet hands for every step. Dough doesn't stick to wet skin.

1

Build the levain

6:30am
  1. Mix 15g starter + 30g warm water + 30g flour.
  2. Cover and keep warm (~80°F / 27°C). An oven with just the light on works.
  3. Mark the level with a rubber band.
Ready when: more than tripled, domed, bubbly, smells like yogurt. A spoonful floats in water.
5–6 hours
2

Autolyse

9:30am
  1. 253g warm water + 255g bread flour + 70g whole wheat.
  2. Stir until no dry flour is left, scraping down the sides.
  3. Cover with a shower cap.
Why: soaking the flour starts gluten development before you do any work.
Rest 3 hours (30–60 min with weaker flour)
3

Add the levain

12:30pm
  1. Spoon 65g levain on top (keep a little back to feed your starter).
  2. With a wet hand, spread it across the surface.
  3. Pinch and squeeze it through until it's even.
Feels like: shaggy, sticky and messy at first. That's normal.
Rest 30 min
4

Add the salt

1:00pm
  1. Sprinkle 8g fine sea salt over the surface.
  2. Dimple it in with wet fingertips.
  3. Fold the dough over itself for 4–5 minutes.
Why: salt tightens the gluten, adds strength and slows fermentation a little.
Rest 30 min
Float test: drop a teaspoon of levain into water. Floats = ready. Sinks = give it more time.

Step by step · Part 2

Building strength

Each fold lines up the gluten. After each rest the dough should feel a bit stronger and hold its shape a bit better.

5

Light fold on the counter

1:30pm
  1. Mist the counter. Scrape the dough out of the bowl.
  2. With a wet bench knife, lift the top edge and fold it down over the dough.
  3. Repeat on all four sides, round it up, back in the bowl.
Check: your first real feel of the dough. Slack or springy? Make a note.
Rest 30 min
6

Lamination: the big stretch

2:00pm
  1. Mist the counter and tip the dough out upside down.
  2. With wet hands, pull gently from the centre outward into a thin rectangle. Don't pull from the edges, and don't tear it.
  3. Fold left third in, right third in, then top down and bottom up. Pop any big air pockets.
Photo moment: hold it up to the light and look for the windowpane.
Rest 45 min
7

Coil folds ×3

2:45 · 3:30 · 4:15pm
  1. With wet hands, reach under the middle of the dough.
  2. Lift slowly until the far end lets go and tucks under itself.
  3. Turn the bowl 90° and repeat on all four sides.
Good sign: the dough stops spreading to the corners and stays rounded at the edges.
Rest 45 min between folds · add 1–2 extra folds if it still feels loose

Step by step · Part 3

Reading the dough & shaping

Temperature controls the clock

Bulk time from adding the levain, at 20% levain

Warmer kitchen = shorter bulk. Cooler kitchen = longer bulk. Watch the dough, not the clock.

Is bulk done? (~6:30pm)

  • About 50–60% bigger than when you added the levain (no more)
  • Puffy and light, growing up as much as out
  • Rounded, domed edges
  • Bubbles along the sides and on top
  • Jiggles when you shake the bowl
Very sticky and slack? It's gone too far. Shape it right away and shorten bulk next time.
Tip: a straight-sided tub with a tape mark shows the 50% rise.

8 · Shape into a batard

6:30pm
1 · Flip outFloured counter, top side down
2 · Sides inFold onto the centre, pat down
3 · Roll downTuck firmly with your thumbs
4 · SealPinch the seam and the ends
5 · Seam upRice flour, into the banneton
The more you pat down, the more even the crumb. Leave a few bubbles for an open, holey crumb.
Final proof: 10–20 min at room temp, then a very cold fridge (38°F / 3°C) for 12–16 hours. Cover it.

Bake day

Bake straight from the fridge. Cold dough is easier to score and springs better. Your loaf has been in since 6:30pm last night.

TimeStepDetail
7:00Preheat500°F / 260°C for 60 min with the Dutch oven (lid on) on the lowest rack, on a broiler tray or two stacked sheet trays so the bottom doesn't burn. Dough stays in the fridge.
8:00Flip & scoreSemolina on the dough, parchment on top, flip it out. One confident line, ½" deep at 45°.
8:02LoadDutch oven out with gloves. Lower the loaf in on the parchment, lid on, back in.
8:02Lid on · 20 minTurn down to 485°F / 250°C.
8:22Lid off · 20–25 minTurn down to 450°F / 230°C. Bake until deep brown. Darker = more flavour.
8:45CoolOnto a rack. Listen to it crackle. Wait at least 1 hour before slicing; it's still cooking inside.

Scoring

Top: one off-centre curve. Side: 45°, ½" deep. A shallow angle lifts a flap that bakes into the "ear".

Is it done?

  • Deep chestnut brown, blistered crust
  • Hollow sound when you tap the base
  • Internal temperature ≥ 205°F / 96°C

When things go sideways

Troubleshooting

Every loaf teaches you something. Cut it in half, look at the crumb, and decide what to change next time.

Just rightTall, even, open, glossy holes

Keeping it alive

Caring for your starter

Flour, water and a colony of wild yeast and bacteria. Feed it and it'll keep going for years.

Pick your rhythm

Weekly bakerDaily baker
LivesFridgeCounter
FeedOnce a weekEvery 12–24h
Ratio1 : 2 : 21 : 1 : 1
Before a bakeFeed the night before, build levain in the morningUse at its peak

A healthy starter…

  • Doubles or more within 4–8 hours of a feed
  • Looks domed and bubbly at its peak
  • Smells like yogurt or apple. Very sour or nail polish = hungry
  • Passes the float test
Grey liquid on top ("hooch")? It's hungry, not dead. Pour it off and feed.
